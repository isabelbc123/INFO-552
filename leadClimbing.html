What is Lead Climbing?

Lead climbing is a type of rock climbing where the climber goes ahead of the belayer, placing anchors as they ascend the wall. It is often done outdoors because it allows for climbers to take a route without having to anchor a rope beforehand. However, it can also be done indoors, which is reccomeded when learning to lead climb.

How to Place Gear

  • Keep the rope between you and the rock at all times.
  • Make sure the rock is solid wherever you place your gear.
  • Learn which gear is best for each location. As a general rule of thumb, use cams for parallel cracks, and use nuts in cracks that get smaller at the bottom.
  • While learning, do a mock climb. This means that you can lead climb, while also attached to a top rope in order to mitigate risk while practicing.

Proper Belaying Techniques for Lead Climbing

  • Belaying a lead climber is different from top rope because it involves letting out slack, as opposed to taking it in.
  • Because of more forceful falls, lead belayers often get pulled up into the wall when a climber falls. When this happens, belayers should bend their knees and place their feet in front of them to brace for impact.
  • Just like top rope belaying, always keep your hand on the brake rope.
  • It's important to manage your slack properly. You want just enough so that your climber can continue climbing, but not too much as that will make falls more dangerous.

Falling Safely

  • Yell "falling" and look below you for any obstructions.
  • Bend your legs and keep your arms by your chest.
  • Don't try to grab anything..
  • Land softly against the wall with your feet in front of you and try to let your legs absorb the shock.
